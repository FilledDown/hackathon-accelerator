# Judging Psychology

Practical facts about how hackathon judging tends to go. These are working assumptions, not laws. Adjust them to the format this event actually uses.

## The conditions judges work in
- Judges often see 20–100+ projects. In expo formats they spend roughly 3–7 minutes per project, and on Devpost-only judging they may spend 1–3 minutes reading. **The first 30 seconds frame everything after.**
- They are tired, they compare projects against each other, and they remember *moments* rather than feature lists.
- Deliberation often comes down to "Which ones do we remember? Which ones actually worked?"

## Implications
1. **Lead with the problem and the person**, in one sentence, then show the product within 30–60 seconds.
2. **Have one memorable moment.** It's the differentiator, shown live, and it should be something a judge could describe to another judge in a single sentence ("the one where the app listens to your piano and highlights the bar you rushed").
3. **Show, don't claim.** Every claim in the pitch needs a matching moment in the demo or a visible piece of the architecture.
4. **Make difficulty visible.** Judges can't see hard work they aren't told about. Spend 15–30 seconds on "the hard part and how we solved it."
5. **Make the sponsor tech essential and named.** Sponsor judges look for their product doing something only it can do.
6. **Reliability beats ambition.** A crashed live demo usually loses to a smaller demo that works. Have a fallback (see demo-engineering.md).
7. **Anticipate the skeptical question.** Common ones: "What happens when the AI is wrong?", "How is this different from X?", "What did you build this weekend versus before?", "Who would pay?", "How does it scale?"

## Judge personas (for the Phase 8 simulation)
- **Technical judge** (engineer): probes architecture and depth, checks whether claims are real, looks at the repo.
- **Sponsor judge**: cares about how deeply and correctly their API is used, and whether it's showcase-worthy.
- **Impact / business judge**: who is the user, evidence of pain, viability, ethics.
- **Design / UX judge**: first impression, flow, consistency, accessibility, mobile.
- **Generalist / organizer judge**: theme fit, rules compliance, completeness, presentation clarity.

Each simulated judge scores against the *official* rubric, from that judge's perspective, and must name: the top strength, the top weakness, one question they would ask, and whether they would argue for this project in deliberation.

## Ethics
Optimize what you communicate and how you present it, never what's true. No misleading demos, made-up metrics, fake testimonials or hidden pre-built code. Besides being wrong, they're disqualification risks.
